Series Two — What Writing Through an Uncertain Season Taught Me

Entrepreneurship · 16 August 2026 · 6 min read

Thirty more posts, written through a genuinely tense few months. Here is what changed in the writing, the business, and the thinking — and where this goes next.

A Different Series Than the First

The first thirty-day series was about proving a system — that I could publish daily and that consistency builds a body of work. This second series set out with a harder brief: keep the five pillars, but write them through a season where the world was visibly tense — oil prices jumping on strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, the rupee under pressure, businesses everywhere gone cautious. The question underneath all thirty posts was really one question: how does a small Indian business actually navigate a world it cannot control?

What the Geopolitics Thread Taught Me

Writing the "Business & Geopolitics" posts changed how I run my own studio. Forcing myself to trace the line from a distant strait to my own fuel costs, gear prices, and client confidence made abstract headlines concrete — and made the response obvious. Every one of those posts landed on the same handful of moves: keep a buffer, diversify what you depend on, price with margin, control your core assets, and earn across more than one basket. Not because they are clever, but because they are the only things actually within a small business's control when the big variables are not.

What Changed in the Craft

Writing across six categories instead of five stretched me. The geopolitics posts demanded a different kind of care — staying practical and grounded, never pretending to be a foreign-policy expert, always pulling the abstract back to something a photographer in Pune could actually do on Monday. That discipline of translation — big event to small action — turned out to be the most useful writing skill of the whole series, and one I will keep using regardless of topic.

The Honest Business Reflection

A tense season is a stress test, and this one confirmed the thesis of both series: the businesses that hold up are the resilient ones, not the biggest or the busiest. My own studio felt the caution — some projects postponed, some budgets tightened — and the buffers, the diversified income, and the steady visibility this blog itself creates were exactly what made that a manageable dip rather than a crisis. I wrote the resilience posts as advice; I also lived them as the author, and they held.

Where This Goes Next

As before, I am not continuing at a daily pace — the sustainable rhythm remains a few substantial posts a week. But the "Business & Geopolitics" thread stays, because the world is not getting simpler and small businesses deserve someone translating it into practical terms rather than panic. Sixty posts in, this blog has become what I hoped: not a content machine, but a genuine record of thinking clearly about craft and business in real conditions. If you have read any part of these thirty, thank you. The next one is already planned.

You cannot control the season you build in. You can only control how you build. Sixty posts have convinced me that is not a limitation to resent — it is the entire craft of running a business, in any weather.
