Why Most People Are Underinsured — And Don&#x27;t Know It

Insurance & Finance · 15 June 2026 · 5 min read

Having a policy is not the same as being protected. Most people buy the minimum and hope for the best.

A Problem Most People Do Not Know They Have

If you asked the average insured Indian whether they are adequately covered, most would say yes. They have a policy. They pay the premium. They feel protected. What most of them have never done is calculate whether the sum assured would actually be sufficient to protect the people who depend on them — for long enough, against realistic expenses, in the absence of their income.

This is not their fault. Insurance is sold, not bought. Policies are recommended based on what the agent can sell and what the buyer can afford to pay in premiums, not based on what the family actually needs. The result is a country full of people who are insured but not protected.

The Coverage Calculation

The standard starting formula is 10 to 15 times your annual income. If you earn ₹8 lakh per year, your life insurance cover should be between ₹80 lakh and ₹1.2 crore. The idea is that if invested conservatively, this lump sum can replace your income for a decade — long enough for your family to restructure their financial life without you.

But income replacement is only the floor, not the ceiling. To calculate your true coverage need, add to the income-replacement figure: all outstanding debts (home loan balance, car loan, personal loans), the full projected cost of your children's education through graduation, any financial commitments to ageing parents, and a buffer for medical emergencies in the first two to three years after loss.

Add those together. That is your actual coverage need. For most people in their thirties with a home loan and children, this number is well above ₹1 crore. Many have policies for ₹10 to ₹25 lakh. The gap between what they have and what they need is significant.

The ₹10 Lakh Policy Problem

A ₹10 lakh sum assured is the most common policy I see when I sit down with new clients. It sounds like a large number until you run the calculation. At ₹7 lakh annual income, ₹10 lakh covers roughly 17 months of salary equivalent. After that, the family is entirely on their own — managing a home loan, school fees, living expenses, with no income replacement.

These policies were often purchased based on what the premium felt manageable, not based on what the family needed. A term insurance policy providing ₹1 crore of cover costs a 28-year-old roughly ₹700 to ₹1,000 per month — less than most people spend on dining out. The premium is affordable. The coverage gap it closes is enormous.

Term Insurance vs Endowment — The Honest Version

Term insurance does one thing: it pays your nominated beneficiaries a lump sum if you die within the policy term. It does nothing else. It does not save. It does not invest. It does not give you anything back if you survive. This simplicity is why it is cheap and why it provides maximum cover for minimum premium.

Endowment plans and ULIPs combine insurance with an investment component. Part of your premium goes toward life cover and part goes toward a fund that grows over time. At maturity, you receive a lump sum. These products are more expensive for the same level of cover because you are paying for both the insurance and the investment management.

For pure protection — ensuring your family is financially secure if you are gone — term insurance is the rational choice. For specific goals that combine protection with forced savings, endowment products can be appropriate. The mistake is buying an endowment plan as if it were a protection product. The cover amount is usually too low for the premium paid.

When to Start

The earlier you buy, the lower your premium for the same cover amount. A ₹1 crore term plan at age 25 costs significantly less than the same plan bought at 35 — and you are locked into that premium for the full term. Every year you wait, the same level of cover costs more and your window of insurability may narrow if your health changes.

This is not a sales argument — it is arithmetic. The cost of waiting is real and compounding. The conversation about life insurance should happen before you need it, not when you are already in a health crisis or when your income has dropped.

Insurance is not an investment. It is a guarantee that the people who depend on you will not suffer financially if you are gone. The question is not whether you can afford the premium. The question is whether your family can afford the gap if you do not have it.

One Practical Step

Check your current policy document. Find the sum assured. Divide it by your annual income. If the result is less than 10, you are underinsured.
