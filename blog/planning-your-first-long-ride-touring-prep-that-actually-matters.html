Planning Your First Long Ride — Touring Prep That Actually Matters

Motorcycle Education · 12 August 2026 · 6 min read

The dream is the open road. The reality of a first long tour is decided days before you leave — in the preparation most new riders skip in their excitement to just go.

Adventure Is Well-Managed Boredom

Every experienced tourer will tell you the same thing: the great rides are the uneventful ones. The stories about breakdowns in the middle of nowhere and nights stranded are fun to tell and miserable to live, and almost all of them trace back to preparation skipped in the rush to leave. A first long ride is not made memorable by improvising through disasters. It is made memorable by the preparation that lets you simply enjoy the road.

The Machine

Before any long ride, the bike gets a proper service, not a hopeful glance: fresh oil if due, chain cleaned and correctly tensioned, tyres with real tread and correct pressure, brakes with pad life to spare, all lights and the battery healthy. A breakdown near home is an inconvenience; the same breakdown 400 km out on an unfamiliar highway is a genuinely different problem. The pre-ride check from earlier in this series scales up here into a full inspection.

The Rider

Long-distance riding is an endurance activity, and new tourers dramatically underestimate the fatigue. Plan realistic daily distances — far less than you think you can do — with breaks every 100 to 150 km, because tired riders make the mistakes that end tours. Gear matters more over distance: a full day in the sun, wind, and possibly rain punishes anyone underdressed. Hydration and food are not luxuries; a dehydrated, under-fuelled rider loses concentration exactly when a long day demands the most of it.

The Plan

Know your fuel range and where the gaps are — long stretches without pumps exist and surprise people. Carry basic tools, a tyre repair kit, and the knowledge to use them. Keep essential documents and some cash for places cards and networks do not reach. Share your route and expected timing with someone at home. And in an uncertain year, a note the previous posts have hammered: do not assume constant connectivity or that every service will be available — a little self-sufficiency turns a problem into a footnote.

The best long rides feel effortless precisely because so much effort went in before the first kilometre. Prepare thoroughly, and the road gives you what you came for — the ride itself, undisturbed.
