How to Shoot in Manual Mode — Step by Step

Tutorials · 22 July 2026 · 5 min read

Manual mode is where photography stops happening to you and starts being something you decide. The morning edition explained the exposure triangle; here is how to actually use it.

From Theory to the Dial

An earlier post explained aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. This is the hands-on follow-up: a repeatable sequence for setting them yourself, so manual mode feels like control rather than guesswork.

The Order of Operations

1. Decide the look first. Blurred background or everything sharp? That sets your aperture — wide (f/1.8–f/2.8) for separation, narrow (f/8–f/11) for front-to-back sharpness. 2. Set shutter speed for your subject. Still subject and steady hands: 1/125s is fine. Movement to freeze: 1/500s or faster. Below roughly 1/60s handheld, expect blur. 3. Fix ISO last. Start at 100. Then look at your camera's light meter — the little scale showing over/under exposure — and raise ISO only until the meter reads zero. 4. Read the meter, adjust, shoot. Too dark? Raise ISO or slow the shutter. Too bright? Lower ISO or speed the shutter, protecting the aperture look you chose.

The Practice Drill

Photograph one static object in changing light — near a window across an afternoon. Keep the aperture fixed; balance exposure using only shutter and ISO. In an hour the relationship stops being three numbers and becomes instinct. That instinct is the entire point of manual mode.

Manual mode is not harder than auto — it is just a fixed order of four decisions. Make them in sequence enough times and the camera finally does what you meant, not what it guessed.
