Editing Your First Photo — A Lightroom Starter

Tutorials · 18 July 2026 · 4 min read

The afternoon edition kicks off with a hands-on tutorial. Editing intimidates beginners, but 90% of a great edit comes from five sliders you can learn in one sitting.

A Second Post, Every Afternoon

This is the first of a new daily 4 PM edition — quicker reads focused on tutorials, tech, general knowledge, and world events, alongside the morning craft-and-business posts. We start where most people feel stuck: editing.

The Five Sliders That Do Most of the Work

Open any raw photo and ignore the hundred controls. Reach for five, in order. Exposure sets overall brightness — get the photo to feel right before anything else. Contrast or better, the pair of Highlights (down) and Shadows (up), recovers detail in the bright and dark areas that the camera clipped. Whites and Blacks set your true endpoints — nudge until the very brightest and darkest points are just shy of pure. Vibrance lifts muted colours without making skin tones radioactive the way Saturation does. Texture/Clarity, used gently, adds bite.

The Workflow

Always edit in that order — global brightness, then recovery, then endpoints, then colour, then detail. Editing top-to-bottom means each adjustment builds on a stable base instead of fighting the last one. And edit to taste on a calibrated-enough screen, then walk away and look again tomorrow; fresh eyes catch the over-editing that tired eyes call "punchy."

You do not need to master a hundred sliders. Five, used in the right order, turn a flat raw file into a finished photograph — and everything else is refinement.
