Inflation and Interest Rates — Explained Without the Jargon

General Knowledge · 1 August 2026 · 5 min read

Two words dominate every economic headline, and most people nod along without a clear picture. Understanding them is basic financial literacy that directly affects your money.

Inflation — Your Money Shrinking Quietly

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time, which means each rupee buys a little less than it used to. A moderate amount is normal and even healthy. The problem is what it does silently: money sitting idle loses purchasing power every year. Cash under a mattress is not "safe" — it is slowly melting. This single fact is why simply saving is not enough and why investing, which the morning edition touched on, matters.

Interest Rates — The Price of Money

An interest rate is the cost of borrowing money, or the reward for lending it. Central banks — in India, the RBI — raise and lower a benchmark rate to steer the economy. When they raise rates, borrowing gets costlier, spending slows, and inflation cools. When they cut rates, borrowing gets cheaper, activity speeds up. Your loan EMIs, your savings returns, and business borrowing costs all move with these decisions.

Why They Move Together

Here is the link that ties the headlines together: when inflation runs hot — as global energy shocks this year have pushed it — central banks tend to raise interest rates to cool it down. That is why "tensions push up oil, oil pushes up inflation, banks raise rates, your EMI rises" is a single connected chain, not four separate news stories. Reading it as one chain is what basic economic literacy actually buys you.

Inflation shrinks your idle money; interest rates are the lever used to control it. Understand those two and half of all economic news stops being noise and starts being a story you can follow.
