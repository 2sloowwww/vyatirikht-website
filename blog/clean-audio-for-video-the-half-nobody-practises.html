Clean Audio for Video — The Half Nobody Practises

Tutorials · 3 August 2026 · 5 min read

Viewers forgive imperfect video. They will not forgive bad audio — they simply leave. Yet audio is where most creators put the least effort. Here is how to fix that fast.

Audio Is Half Your Video — and the Half People Judge

Studies and instinct agree: audiences tolerate rough footage but abandon rough sound within seconds. Bad audio reads subconsciously as "unprofessional" and "not worth my time." The good news is that clean audio is far cheaper and more learnable than clean video — it is mostly about a few simple habits.

The Three Rules That Fix Most Problems

1. Get the mic close. The single biggest improvement. A cheap microphone a foot from your mouth beats an expensive one across the room, because closeness captures your voice and rejects the room. Never rely on the camera's built-in mic for anything that matters. 2. Kill the room. Hard bare walls create echo; record in smaller, softer spaces — soft furnishings, curtains, even a cupboard of clothes tame reflections dramatically. 3. Control the noise floor. Turn off fans, AC, and anything humming before you roll; background noise is far easier to prevent than to remove later.

The Fix-It-in-Post Trap

Software can reduce noise and balance levels, but it cannot rescue audio that was recorded badly at the source — cleaning up bad sound always leaves it sounding processed and thin. Spend your effort at capture: close mic, quiet room, levels set so the loudest parts do not distort. Get that right and post-production becomes a light polish instead of a rescue.

People watch bad video with good audio and leave good video with bad audio. Close mic, quiet room, controlled levels — three habits that fix the half of video most creators ignore.
