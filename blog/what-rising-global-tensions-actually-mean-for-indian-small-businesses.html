What Rising Global Tensions Actually Mean for Indian Small Businesses

Business & Geopolitics · 18 July 2026 · 6 min read

The headlines feel far away — strikes in West Asia, oil prices jumping, trade routes under pressure. They are not far away. They reach your business through fuel, imports, and customer confidence.

Series Two Begins — With the Topic Nobody in Business Talks About

I ended the last 30-day series saying I would slow down. Instead, here is series two — because the months since have given all of us something worth writing about. As I write this in July 2026, tensions in West Asia have pushed Brent crude past $80 a barrel after strikes near the Strait of Hormuz, and every business owner I work with has felt some version of the same nervousness: what does any of this mean for me?

This series keeps the five pillars this blog is built on — photography, motorcycles, social media, money, and building businesses — and adds a sixth thread: how a small Indian business actually navigates a tense world. Not foreign policy commentary. Practical impact.

The Three Channels That Reach You

Fuel. India imports most of its crude oil. When tensions push global prices up, transport costs follow — your courier rates, your travel costs to shoots, the price of getting products to customers. It arrives with a lag, but it arrives.

Imports. Cameras, phones, electronics, packaging materials, even some printing supplies — priced in dollars, shipped through routes that get more expensive when the world gets tense. A weaker rupee amplifies this.

Confidence. The least visible and most powerful channel. When headlines turn dark, customers postpone non-essential spending — the wedding shoot can wait, the rebrand can wait. Uncertainty taxes discretionary businesses first.

What You Can Actually Do

You cannot hedge crude futures from a studio in Pune. You can do three unglamorous things. Keep a cash buffer — uncertain months punish businesses running on empty tanks. Price with room — quotes that assume today's costs hold for six months are quiet losses waiting to happen. Diversify income — one client, one platform, one service is fragility pretending to be focus.

Geopolitics is not something that happens to other people. It is the water your business swims in. You cannot control the tide — but you can absolutely learn to read it.
