Why Data Is Called "The New Oil"

Business & Geopolitics · 10 August 2026 · 5 min read

You generate it constantly and give it away for free. Understanding why data became one of the most valuable resources on Earth changes how you think about every free app.

The Resource You Produce Without Noticing

Every search, tap, purchase, location, and pause on a video generates data. Individually trivial; in aggregate, extraordinarily valuable — enough that "data is the new oil" became the defining phrase of the digital economy. Understanding why demystifies a lot of how the modern internet actually works.

Why It Is So Valuable

Data's value is in prediction. Enough of it lets companies predict what you will buy, watch, or believe — and prediction is what advertisers pay enormous sums for, what trains AI systems, and what powers the personalised feeds that keep you scrolling. This is why so many services are "free": you are not the customer, you are the supplier. Your data is the product being refined and sold, and the service is the machine that extracts it.

Why It Is Geopolitical

Like oil, data has become strategic. Governments increasingly demand that data about their citizens be stored within national borders (data localisation), worry about foreign companies and states accessing sensitive information, and treat control of large data flows as national power. India, with one of the world's largest populations of internet users, sits at the centre of these debates. For an individual, the practical takeaway is simply awareness: free services are paid for with your data, and that exchange is worth making consciously rather than by default.

If a digital service is free, your data is the price — and that data is valuable enough to reshape economies and provoke nations. "The new oil" is not a metaphor to admire; it is a transaction to understand.
