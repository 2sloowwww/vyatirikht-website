The Pre-Ride Check — Two Minutes That Prevent Most Breakdowns

Motorcycle Education · 5 August 2026 · 5 min read

Most roadside breakdowns and a surprising share of accidents trace back to something a two-minute check would have caught. Riders skip it because nothing goes wrong — until it does.

The Habit That Separates Riders From Passengers

Every pilot walks around the aircraft before flight. Not because they expect a problem — because the cost of missing one is too high to leave to chance. A motorcycle deserves the same two minutes, and the riders who build the habit almost never find themselves stranded or surprised by a failure that had been announcing itself for days.

The Simple Framework: T-CLOCS, Shortened

You do not need the full checklist every day. A daily version covers the essentials in under two minutes. Tyres: a glance for obvious low pressure, a look for embedded nails or fresh cuts. Controls: squeeze both brakes — firm, not spongy; twist the throttle — snaps back cleanly. Lights: headlight, brake light, indicators, all working, because they are how traffic sees your intentions. Oil and leaks: a quick look under the bike for fresh drips. Chain: roughly correct tension, not bone-dry.

The Weekly Deeper Look

Once a week, go further: tyre pressure with an actual gauge (not a thumb), tread depth, brake pad wear, coolant level on liquid-cooled bikes, and cable condition. Ten minutes weekly plus two minutes daily is the entire maintenance-awareness routine that keeps most riders out of most trouble — and turns you into someone who notices a problem forming rather than someone it happens to.

Why Riders Skip It — And Why That Logic Fails

The check gets skipped precisely because it usually finds nothing. But "usually nothing" is the wrong measure — the value is in the rare morning it finds the soft tyre before the highway, the dragging brake before the ghat, the frayed cable before it snaps mid-junction. You are not checking because you expect failure today. You are checking because the one day it matters, nothing else will have warned you.

The two minutes before you ride are the cheapest insurance in motorcycling. Skipping them saves two minutes on the 364 days nothing was wrong, and costs you dearly on the one day something was.
