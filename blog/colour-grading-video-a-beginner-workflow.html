Colour Grading Video — A Beginner Workflow

Tutorials · 26 July 2026 · 5 min read

The difference between footage that looks "homemade" and footage that looks "produced" is usually grading. Here is a simple, repeatable order to get there.

Grade in Two Stages, Always

Amateur grading fails because people jump straight to a "look." Professionals split it into two stages: correction first (making the footage technically neutral and consistent), then grading (adding a deliberate mood). Do them in that order and even simple tools produce clean results.

Stage One — Correction

Clip by clip, balance the basics: set exposure so nothing important is crushed to black or blown to white, fix white balance so whites are actually white (not orange or blue), and match shots that were filmed moments apart but look different. The goal here is boring on purpose — neutral, consistent, correct. Skipping this and grading straight away is why beginner footage looks uneven from cut to cut.

Stage Two — The Look

Now add character, gently. Lift or crush the shadows slightly for cinematic contrast, push the overall colour toward a mood (teal-and-orange is a cliché for a reason — it flatters skin), and reduce saturation a touch so it reads as intentional rather than loud. Apply the same look across every clip so the whole video feels like one piece. Then dial the intensity down until you almost cannot tell it is there — restraint is what separates a grade from a filter.

Correct first, grade second, and keep the look subtle. Most "cinematic" footage is not heavily stylised — it is carefully corrected, gently graded, and consistent from shot to shot.
