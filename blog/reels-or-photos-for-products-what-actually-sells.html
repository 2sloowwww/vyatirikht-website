Reels or Photos for Products — What Actually Sells

Photography · 3 August 2026 · 5 min read

Every client asks the same question now: "should we just do reels?" The honest answer is that reels and photos do completely different jobs — and confusing them wastes money.

The False Either-Or

Since reels took over reach, clients have started treating product photography as the outdated option — "photos don't perform anymore." The data from my own client accounts says something more precise: reels dominate discovery, photos dominate decision. A jewellery reel reaches ten times the strangers; the catalogue photo is what those strangers study before they message you. One fills the shop with visitors. The other closes the sale. Cutting either breaks the machine.

What Reels Do Best

Reels are reach engines: platforms push them to non-followers, which makes them the cheapest cold-audience tool a small brand has. They carry motion, transformation, and personality — the unboxing, the light hitting a stone as it turns, the before/after. Their weakness is precision: a reel cannot be zoomed, studied, or compared. Nobody buys a ₹40,000 ring off fifteen frames a second.

What Photos Do Best

Photos are trust engines. They live where decisions happen: the product grid a customer scrolls after the reel hooked them, the WhatsApp catalogue, the website, the print. They reward scrutiny — texture, detail, true colour — and their production cost per asset is far lower. A strong catalogue photo works for a year across five platforms. A reel works for seventy-two hours of algorithm attention.

The Budget Split That Works

For product businesses I now recommend roughly 60/40: reels for discovery velocity (two to three a week, hook-first, shot in batches), photos for the permanent decision layer (a proper catalogue refreshed each season, consistent lighting and background so the grid reads as one brand). And one production trick that halves cost: shoot both in the same session — the styled setup for the hero photo is already lit for fifteen seconds of reel footage.

Reels bring the crowd. Photos close the customer. Asking which one sells is asking whether the shop needs a signboard or a counter — it needs both, doing their own jobs.
