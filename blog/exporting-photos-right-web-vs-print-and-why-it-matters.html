Exporting Photos Right — Web vs Print, and Why It Matters

Tutorials · 11 August 2026 · 4 min read

You finished editing. The last step trips up more people than any other: exporting. Get it wrong and your work looks soft online or prints muddy. Here is the simple rule set.

The Overlooked Final Step

Export is where good edits go to die. The same photo needs completely different treatment for a phone screen versus a printed frame, and using one file for both guarantees one of them looks wrong. Two short recipes solve it.

For Web and Social

Screens are low-resolution and bandwidth matters, so resize down — a few thousand pixels on the long edge is plenty; uploading a massive full-resolution file just gets crushed by the platform anyway, often worse than if you had resized cleanly yourself. Export as JPEG at high (not maximum) quality, in the sRGB colour space — this is critical, because sRGB is what browsers and apps assume, and exporting in a wider space is why photos sometimes look dull or oddly coloured once uploaded. Apply a touch of sharpening for screen at the end.

For Print

Print is the opposite: send the full resolution, because paper resolves far more detail than a screen and downsizing throws away quality you paid for. Use a high-quality format, sharpen a little more aggressively (print softens images slightly), and if your printer specifies a colour profile, use it. When in doubt, ask the print service what they want rather than guessing.

Editing is not finished until you export correctly. Small sRGB JPEGs for screens; full-resolution, print-sharpened files for paper. The same file for both is the quiet reason so much good work looks worse than it is.
